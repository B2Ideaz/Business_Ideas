Definitions

Basic

Business ⌄

An activity or organization that provides value in exchange for money.

A business creates products or services that customers are willing to pay for. Businesses can be small, large, local, or global.

Company ⌄

A legal entity used to operate a business.

A company can own property, hire employees, sign contracts, earn revenue, and incur expenses.

Product ⌄

Something a business sells.

Products can be physical goods, digital goods, software, subscriptions, or anything else customers pay for.

Service ⌄

Work performed for a customer.

Unlike products, services are usually activities performed for someone, such as consulting, accounting, or lawn care.

Customer ⌄

A person or organization that buys a product or service.

Customers are the source of revenue for most businesses.

Revenue ⌄

The money a business earns from sales.

Revenue is the total amount of money generated before expenses are deducted.

Cost ⌄

The money spent operating a business.

Costs include rent, salaries, software, marketing, equipment, and other expenses.

Profit ⌄

The money left after costs are deducted.

Profit = Revenue − Costs

Businesses generally aim to generate profit over time.

Startup ⌄

A business designed for rapid growth.

Startups often focus on innovation, technology, and scalability.

Market Share ⌄

The percentage of a market controlled by a business.

Market share helps measure a company's position relative to competitors.

Advanced

Subscription Model ⌄

Customers pay recurring fees for access.

Common among SaaS companies, streaming services, and memberships.

Freemium Model ⌄

Basic features are free while advanced features require payment.

This model helps attract users before converting some into paying customers.

Marketplace Model ⌄

Connect buyers and sellers and earn a fee.

The platform itself often does not own the goods being sold.

Advertising Model ⌄

Revenue comes from displaying advertisements.

Businesses monetize attention rather than directly selling products to users.

Licensing Model ⌄

Others pay to use intellectual property.

Common with software, patents, trademarks, and media.

B2B vs B2C ⌄

Different customer types.

B2B: Business sells to other businesses.

B2C: Business sells directly to consumers.

Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) ⌄

The cost of acquiring a new customer.

CAC measures how much a company spends on sales and marketing to gain one customer.

Lifetime Value (LTV) ⌄

The total value a customer generates over their relationship with a business.

LTV helps businesses understand how much they can spend acquiring customers.

Scalability ⌄

The ability to grow without costs increasing at the same rate.

Software businesses are often highly scalable because serving additional customers costs very little.

Competitive Advantage ⌄

Something that makes a business difficult to compete against.

Examples include strong brands, patents, network effects, economies of scale, or unique technology.
